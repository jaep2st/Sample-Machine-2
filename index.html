Where the flip modes come from

Each mode is built on a technique the legends are known for. Names describe the technique; the producers are credited here, not affiliated with this app.

Soul Speed-Up

Speeds the loop up like a turntable, so pitch and tempo rise together. The sped-up soul sound credited to RZA and Just Blaze and taken mainstream by Kanye West around 2003.

Source

Screwed

Slows the loop to around 60–70 BPM, pitch dropping with it, and runs the last phrase back. After Houston's DJ Screw, who invented chopped and screwed in the early '90s.

Source

Pitch-Down

Drops the loop three semitones, tape-style, and darkens the top end: the moody, pitched-down jazz and soul loops of '90s New York.

Source

Micro-Chop Flip

Cuts the loop into 8th notes and re-sequences the punchiest slices into a new phrase, keeping the downbeat. The flip-it-into-something-new approach of DJ Premier, J Dilla and 9th Wonder.

Source

Off-Grid Flip

A flip played with MPC-style 66% swing, slightly rushed snares and loose timing, after the unquantized feel J Dilla built on the MPC 3000.

Source

Stab Kit

Finds the strongest single hit in the chop and pitches it across 8 pads in the song's key, so you can play a new melody, the way DJ Premier built Biggie's "Unbelievable" from one note.

Source

Drum Kit

Pulls single kicks, snares and hats out of a chop so you can program your own pattern. Marley Marl did this with the "Impeach the President" break on MC Shan's "The Bridge" in 1986.

Source

Filtered Bass

A steep low-pass that turns any loop into a bassline, the trick Pete Rock used on his SP-1200 and Akai S950.

Source

SP Grit

Rebuilds the E-mu SP-1200's 26 kHz, 12-bit crunch, the sound of golden-age East Coast hip-hop.

Source

Dusty Loop

Tape saturation, vinyl and a rolled-off top end: the mood-first loops of Madlib's cheap SP-303 and The Alchemist.

Source

Breaks tab

Finds drum-only sections. The most-sampled records ever are mostly drum breaks: "Amen, Brother", "Think (About It)", "Funky Drummer", "Impeach the President".

Source

Open tab

Finds melodic sections with few or no drums, like intros and breakdowns, the raw material of drumless loops by The Alchemist, Daringer and Griselda.

Source
